What if you could strengthen your core, improve your posture, and relieve back pain—all in just two minutes a day? The **Japanese Long Breath Method**, also known as the Ryosuke Breathing Technique, promises exactly that. And unlike many fitness trends that fade quickly, this simple breathing exercise has been practiced in Japan for over a decade.

The method was discovered accidentally by Japanese actor Miki Ryosuke, who was practicing breathing exercises to relieve chronic back pain. What he found surprised him: not only did his back pain improve, but his core became noticeably stronger. The technique requires no equipment, can be done standing or sitting, and takes less time than brewing a cup of tea.

## The Accidental Discovery

Miki Ryosuke, a 55-year-old Japanese actor, wasn't trying to create a fitness method. He was simply following his doctor's advice to do breathing exercises for severe back pain. But after practicing the technique consistently for several weeks, he noticed something remarkable: his waist had gotten smaller, his posture had improved, and his back pain had significantly decreased.

Ryosuke shared his experience, and the technique quickly spread across Japan. His approach became known as the *Long Breath Diet* (ロングブレスダイエット), though the real benefits go far beyond weight management. For seniors especially, the core-strengthening and posture-correcting aspects are what make this method truly valuable.

## How to Do the Japanese Long Breath Method

The beauty of this technique lies in its simplicity. The basic pattern is easy to remember: **inhale for 3 seconds, exhale for 7 seconds**. That's the foundation of the entire method. But the way you position your body and engage your muscles during these breaths is what makes it effective.

### Standing Version (Primary Method)

**Step 1:** Stand with your feet hip-width apart. Place one foot slightly in front of the other, with about 90% of your weight on your back foot. Keep your spine straight and your shoulders relaxed.

**Step 2:** Tighten your buttocks slightly to engage your core and stabilize your pelvis.

**Step 3:** Inhale slowly through your nose for 3 seconds while raising your arms above your head. Feel your chest expand and your ribcage lift.

**Step 4:** Exhale forcefully through your mouth for 7 seconds while contracting all your muscles—especially your abdominals. Imagine you're blowing out candles on a birthday cake while squeezing your belly button toward your spine.

**Step 5:** Repeat this cycle for 2 to 5 minutes. Most people start with 6 repetitions (about 1 minute) and gradually increase.

### Seated Version (For Limited Mobility)

If standing is difficult or you're just getting started, the seated version works equally well for engaging your core muscles.

**How to perform:** Sit on the edge of a sturdy chair with your feet flat on the floor. Place one hand on your lower belly and one on your lower back. Sit tall with your spine straight. Inhale through your nose for 3 seconds, feeling your belly expand against your hand. Then exhale through your mouth for 7 seconds while actively pulling your belly button inward. Feel the contraction with your hand.

The hands provide valuable feedback—you'll know you're doing it correctly when you feel your abdominal muscles working during the exhale.

## Why the Long Breath Method Works

The effectiveness of the Japanese Long Breath Method comes down to what happens when you exhale forcefully while engaging your muscles. This isn't passive breathing—it's an isometric exercise disguised as a breathing technique.

**Core activation:** When you exhale forcefully and pull your belly inward, you're activating your transverse abdominis—the deep core muscle that wraps around your midsection like a corset. This is the same muscle that physical therapists target when treating back pain.

**Diaphragmatic engagement:** The 3-second inhale encourages full diaphragmatic breathing, which many of us lose as we age due to shallow breathing habits. Proper diaphragm function supports core stability and spinal health.

**Posture correction:** The stance and arm positions naturally encourage proper spinal alignment. Practicing this daily can help counteract the forward-hunching posture that often develops with age.

## Specific Benefits for Seniors

While the Long Breath Method benefits people of all ages, it's particularly well-suited for older adults. Here's why:

**Low impact on joints:** Unlike crunches or planks, this exercise puts no pressure on your knees, wrists, or spine. You're simply breathing with intention.

**Builds core strength safely:** A strong core is essential for balance and fall prevention. The Long Breath Method strengthens your deep core muscles without the risks associated with more aggressive ab exercises.

**Supports back health:** Remember, Ryosuke discovered this method while treating back pain. The core engagement and posture correction work together to support your lower back.

**Improves breathing capacity:** Many seniors experience reduced lung capacity over time. The deep breathing patterns in this exercise can help maintain and even improve respiratory function.

**Reduces stress:** The extended exhale (7 seconds) activates your parasympathetic nervous system, promoting relaxation and reducing stress hormones.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Holding your breath:** The transition between inhale and exhale should be smooth. Don't hold your breath at the top—flow directly from the 3-second inhale into the 7-second exhale.

**Forgetting the muscle engagement:** Simply breathing deeply isn't enough. The magic happens when you actively contract your abdominal muscles during the exhale. Think of "squeezing" the air out rather than just letting it escape.

**Starting too intensely:** If you feel dizzy or lightheaded, you're pushing too hard. Start with just 4-5 breath cycles and increase gradually over several weeks.

**Inconsistent practice:** Like any exercise, the Long Breath Method only works if you do it regularly. Two minutes every day is far more effective than ten minutes once a week.

## A Realistic Perspective on Results

Let's be honest about what this method can and can't do. The Japanese Long Breath Method is sometimes marketed as a "miracle weight loss technique," but the science doesn't fully support those claims. While some practitioners report losing inches from their waist, this is likely due to improved posture and core muscle tone rather than fat loss.

What the method *can* reliably deliver: better posture, stronger core muscles, improved breathing habits, reduced back pain, and a daily moment of mindful movement. For seniors, these benefits are genuinely valuable—perhaps more valuable than dramatic weight loss claims.

Think of it as one piece of a healthy lifestyle puzzle. Combined with regular walking, balanced nutrition, and other gentle exercises, the Long Breath Method can be a powerful tool for maintaining your health and independence as you age.

---

## When to Be Cautious

While the Long Breath Method is generally safe, certain conditions warrant caution. If you have uncontrolled high blood pressure, consult your doctor before starting—the breath-holding and muscle contraction can temporarily raise blood pressure. People with respiratory conditions like COPD should also check with their healthcare provider.

If you experience dizziness, stop immediately and sit down. Some lightheadedness is common when you first start (due to increased oxygen intake), but it should pass within a few days of regular practice. If it persists, reduce the intensity or consult a professional.

## Your First Week: A Simple Plan

**Days 1-3:** Practice the seated version only. Do 5 breath cycles (about 50 seconds) twice per day—morning and evening. Focus on getting the timing right: 3 seconds in, 7 seconds out.

**Days 4-5:** If you feel comfortable, try the standing version. Continue with 5-6 breath cycles twice daily. Pay attention to your posture and the feeling of your core engaging during the exhale.

**Days 6-7:** Increase to 8-10 breath cycles per session (about 90 seconds to 2 minutes). Notice how it's becoming more natural.

By the end of the first week, you should have a solid foundation. From there, you can gradually increase to 2-5 minutes per session, once or twice daily.
